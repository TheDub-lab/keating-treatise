# 11th Circuit — Pages and Navigation Investigation

Evidence gathered on the 11th Circuit pages Keating's seminar describes, and
on the actual court framework (so we can separate the navigation from the
source).

---

## 1. The tutorial's actual instructions

Keating's seminar gives this path:

1. Go to "U.S. Courts" (search engine).
2. Type "U.S. Courts".
3. Go to **"the 11th Circuit Court of the United States"** (Circuit 1 thru
   Circuit 11).
4. **"Click on Circuit 7"** → takes you into various courts: Bankruptcy,
   District, etc.
5. Click to **Northern Illinois District Court**.
6. Clerk's Office → box → **Administrative Offices** → **Financial
   Department**.
7. It will talk about the **"Criminal Justice Act"** and **"Optional Bids"**.
8. Go down to **"List of Sureties"** in a Federal District Court.
9. The "List of Sureties" will have **"FMS.Treas.gov"**.
10. Go into Department of Treasury → left side → **"Admitted Reinsure"** →
    **"List of Sureties"** → **"Forms"**.
11. Then you'll see about 300 reinsurance companies, all insurance companies.
12. "Under 750 of the Department of Treasury, they have to be certified
    before they can buy up these Bonds."
13. Click "Forms" → "Miller Act reinsurance" → lists 3 bonds (273, 274, 275).

## 2. What the tutorial actually points to

| Keating's instruction | What it actually is |
|---|---|
| "11th Circuit Court of the United States" | A geographic appellate court (Atlanta, GA). The tutorial uses it as a *wayfinding* entry point into federal court sites. |
| "Click on Circuit 7" | Northern Illinois District Court is in the **Seventh Circuit**, not the Eleventh. The tutorial appears to have reversed the circuit number. |
| "List of Sureties" in a Federal District Court | A real administrative file in many federal district courts. The courts do maintain notice of acceptable sureties under **Treasury Circular 570**, and the AO of U.S. Courts publishes bond rules (Guides to Judiciary Policy Vol. 14, Ch. 6, §§ 610, 620). |
| "Click on FMS.Treas.gov" | The list leads to the U.S. Treasury **Bureau of the Fiscal Service**, which maintains the "List of Admitted Reinsurers" and "List of Certified Companies" under **Department Circular 570**. |
| "Under 750 of the Department of Treasury" | Treasury's beneficiary and contracting regulations are housed within the **Fiscal Service** and the **Bureau of the Fiscal Service** (Treasury). The specific "750" citation is **not verified**. |
| "Miller Act reinsurance... 3 bonds (273, 274, 275)" | SF273 = Reinsurance, SF274 = Performance Bond, SF275 = Payment Bond (Miller Act). These are real GSA/Act standard forms for federal construction contracts. |
| "The U.S. District Court buys all State Court Judgments" | Not established. Asserted in the seminar; no evidentiary record found. |

## 3. What is real and verified

| Item | Status |
|---|---|
| **U.S. Courts of Appeals** exist (12 regional circuits + Federal Circuit) | ✅ Verified |
| **11th Circuit** covers Georgia, Florida, Alabama | ✅ Verified (Atlanta, GA) |
| **Northern Illinois District Court** is in the **Seventh Circuit**, not the 11th | ✅ Verified (misdirection in tutorial) |
| **Administrative Office of the U.S. Courts (AO)** publishes court bond rules | ✅ Verified — *Guide to Judiciary Policy, Vol. 14, Ch. 6 (Bonds, Insurance, Taxes, and Intellectual Property)*, §§ 610, 620 |
| **Treasury Circular 570** — certified sureties for federal bonds | ✅ Verified — U.S. Treasury Bureau of the Fiscal Service |
| **FMS.Treas.gov** leads to Treasury surety/reinsurance materials | ✅ Verified |
| **SF273/274/275** (Miller Act reinsurance/performance/payment bonds) | ✅ Real forms, but for construction/procurement contracts |
| **List of Sureties in a Federal District Court** | ⚠️ Plausible (courts do maintain surety lists under Circular 570), but **not independently confirmed to exist exactly as described** in any public federal court portal |

## 4. What is NOT verified

1. That any federal district court's "Administrative Offices → Financial
   Department" page contains "Criminal Justice Act," "Optional Bids," and a
   "List of Sureties" leading to FMS.Treas.gov. This appears to be a
   **specific file navigation** that has not been directly inspected on live
   court portals.

2. That the "List of Sureties" in a federal district court is a
   "Criminal Justice Act" list, or that it specifically links to
   "Admitted Reinsure → List of Sureties → Forms → Miller Act reinsurance."

3. That the U.S. District Court buys all state court judgments.

4. That "under 750 of the Department of the Treasury" a surety must be
   certified to buy bonds. Treasury's Circular 570 does impose certification,
   but the "750" section citation is not verified.

5. That "admitted reinsurance" companies under 750 are all-insurance
   companies, or that they "can't buy these Bonds unless they are Certified
   by the Secretary of the Treasury" in the exact manner described.

## 5. How to verify this yourself (precisely)

### Option A — Federal court guide (the actual source of court bond rules)
1. Go to **uscourts.gov**.
2. Search for **"Guide to Judiciary Policy Vol. 14"**.
3. Open **Chapter 6: Bonds, Insurance, Taxes, and Intellectual Property**.
4. Read **§ 610 (In General)** and **§ 620 (Sureties and Bond Alternatives)**.
5. Note: the AO of U.S. Courts issues these rules — **not** the 11th
   Circuit, and not a district court clerk's office.

### Option B — Treasury's surety/reinsurance list
1. Go to **fiscal.treasury.gov**.
2. Click **Doing Business with Fiscal Service → Surety Bonds**.
3. Read **Admitted Reinsurers**, **Certified Companies**, and
   **Authorized Reinsurer** pages.
4. Note the structure and number of listed companies.
5. Compare to Keating's description.

### Option C — The 11th Circuit itself
1. Go to **ca11.uscourts.gov** (or the Official U.S. Courts circuit map).
2. Note it is the court of appeals for **Georgia, Florida, Alabama**.
3. Look for any "Financial Department," "List of Sureties," or
   "Admitted Reinsure" page.
4. Record whether any such page exists on the official 11th Circuit portal.

### Option D — Northern Illinois District Court (where the tutorial points)
1. Go to **illinois.north.uscourts.gov** or the federal court finder.
2. Find the Northern District of Illinois.
3. Note its circuit: **Seventh**, not Eleventh.
4. Look for any "Financial Department," "Criminal Justice Act," "Optional
   Bids," or "List of Sureties" page.
5. Record whether it matches the tutorial.

## 6. Bottom line

The 11th Circuit tutorial is a **wayfinding exercise** that uses the
Eleventh Circuit as an entry point into a federal court-to-Treasury
navigational path. The actual source of court bond rules is the
**Administrative Office of the U.S. Courts** (Guide to Judiciary Policy
Vol. 14, Ch. 6), and the actual source of Treasury-certified sureties is the
**Bureau of the Fiscal Service** (Department Circular 570). The tutorial's
claim that the 11th Circuit *hosts* these materials, or that a specific
district court page has the exact structure described, has not been
independently confirmed.
