# Claim-by-Claim Fact-Check

Every substantive claim in Jean Keating's Prison Treatise and the companion
seminar tapes, mapped to its source citation and to the legal reality.

---

## 1. "Statutes are bonds or obligations of record"

**Source cited:** Black's Law Dictionary, 4th ed. revised.

**What's true:** Black's 4th ed. does define "statute" in a paraphrased way
that references "statute merchant" and "statute staple" as historical
security instruments. The treatise itself quotes the relevant definitions.

**What's not true:** Modern statutory law is not, as a matter of general
jurisprudence, a "bond." A statute is a rule of conduct promulgated by a
legislature. Courts apply and enforce statutes as enacted. The historical
"statute merchant/staple" was a medieval mercantile security instrument; it
does not describe a present-day U.S. statute. Mere vocabulary overlap does not
make courts "operating under Statute Law."

**Verdict:** Partially true (the dictionary entry exists), but the inference
that all modern statutes are bonds is **false**.

---

## 2. "The courts operating under Statute Law (Law Merchant / UCC)"

**Source cited:** UCC 1-103; Black's Law Dictionary; "Law Merchant."

**What's true:** UCC 1-103 does say that certain "principles of law and
equity" and "general principles of law" may supplement the code. Commercial
law does exist alongside common law. State rules (e.g., Ohio Revised Code
1775.04) do say rules of law and equity, including the law merchant, govern.

**What's not true:** Federal and state criminal courts do **not** operate
under the Law Merchant or the UCC as their governing framework. The
Constitution, state constitutions, and criminal procedure codes govern
criminal cases. UCC Article 2 (sale of goods), Article 3 (negotiable
instruments), and Article 9 (secured transactions) have specific statutory
scope and do not convert criminal prosecution into commercial debt collection.

**Verdict:** **False.** The UCC is not the governing law of the courtroom.

---

## 3. "The indictment is a negotiable instrument (UCC Article 3)"

**Source cited:** UCC § 3-410 (acceptance), § 3-501 (presentment), § 3-505
(dishonor).

**What's true:** UCC Article 3 governs negotiable instruments (promissory
notes, drafts, checks). It is real and well-defined.

**What's not true:** An indictment is a formal charging document issued by a
grand jury and filed by a prosecutor. It is not a "draft," "note," "check,"
or "bill of exchange." It has no maker, drawee, payee, or acceptor in the
UCC sense. The "Grand Jury Foreman = Drawer/Maker" and "State = Payee" labels
are linguistic analogies, not legal classifications. Courts have rejected the
applicability of the UCC to criminal charging documents. UCC Article 3
nevertheless governs real commercial paper — but indictments are not that.

**Verdict:** **False.** A court-applied legal classification with no basis in
the text or structure of UCC Article 3.

---

## 4. "Three bonds per criminal case (GSA SF24, SF25, SF25A; federal SF273/274/275)"

**Source cited:** GSA Standard Forms 24, 25, 25A (state); SF273, SF274, SF275
(federal), issued by GSA under the FAR (48 CFR § 53.228(a)).

**What's true:** GSA Standard Forms 24 (Bid Bond), 25 (Performance Bond), and
25A (Payment Bond) do exist. Federal GSA equivalents SF273, SF274, SF275 do
exist. The Miller Act requires performance and payment bonds on federal
construction contracts.

**What's not true:** These forms are **construction/procurement surety bonds**
— they guarantee a contractor's building performance and payment of
subcontractors and suppliers. They are **not** criminal charging instruments.
The claim that a judge or clerk "issues" SF24 when a case is lodged is not
how criminal procedure works. There is no requirement in any state or
federal criminal statute that an indictment be accompanied by a GSA bid
bond.

**Verdict:** **False.** GSA standard forms are procurement bonds, not
criminal charging instruments. The "three bonds per case" claim is a
category error.

---

## 5. "Prisons are repository institutions for securities (prisoners = assets)"

**Source cited:** 17 CFR § 450 (SEC depository-bank regulations); Black's
Law Dictionary.

**What's true:** 17 CFR § 450 does regulate "depository banks" (banks that
hold securities in "street name" / DTC). It defines their role in the
securities clearing system.

**What's not true:** Prisons are not depository banks. A repository bank
under 17 CFR 450 holds securities for customers. Prisons house detained
persons under correctional/detention law. The claim that a prison's account
is a SEC-registered depository bank, and that prisoners are "assets" or
"securities" collateralizing the public debt, is not supported by any
regulatory framework (OCC, Fed, FDIC, SEC). The Fiduciary/custodial
capacity of a prison is not a securities repository.

**Verdict:** **False.** A category error — prisons are correctional
facilities, not securities depository banks.

## 6. "Prisoners are the actual reinsurance or surety; the sentence is marketable risk"

**Source cited:** Commercial-insurance concepts (assumed risk), Circular 570.

**What's true:** Insurance/underwriting indeed uses "assumed risk" and
"peril." Treasury Circular 570 does govern certified insurers and
reinsurers on federal bonds.

**What's not true:** Prisoners are not insurance reinsurance. A sentence is
not a "policy" or "premium." There is no regulatory or accounting standard
that values a prisoner's incarceration as a traded risk asset. The private
prison companies (CCA, GEO) are real businesses, but the treatise's claim
that prisoners themselves are the "reinsured" risk on the bid/performance/
payment bonds is not how surety and reinsurance law works. Surety bonds are
issued by licensed surety companies, not by prisoners.

**Verdict:** **False.** Prisons are not insurance/fidelity/reinsurance
vehicles; prisoners are not the surety behind government bonds.

## 7. "All crimes are commercial (27 CFR 72.11)"

**Source cited:** 27 CFR 72.11.

**What's true:** The CFR is organized by subject. Title 27 covers
Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms, and Explosives. Section 72.11 is part of the
Domain of the ATF and relates to **liquor** licensing, not a general
commercial-criminal framework.

**What's not true:** The claim that "27 CFR 72.11, where it says all crimes
are commercial" is a misquote or misreading of a real document. The actual
text of 27 CFR 72.11 does not say that. The general proposition "all crimes
are commercial" is not supported by any statute, regulation, or Supreme
Court ruling.

**Verdict:** **False.** The citation is to the wrong subject matter, and the
proposition it's cited for is not in the text.

---

## 8. "Exemption Priority discharges Public Debt" / "Uniform Exemption Act § 3"

**Source cited:** Commerce/tax codes; "Uniform Exemption Act § 3."

**What's true:** A "Uniform Exemption Act" (or state equivalents) concerns the
exemption of certain property from execution, levy, or judicial lien to
collect a debt. It's a property-law protection, not a debt-discharge
mechanism.

**What's not true:** There is no "Uniform Exemption Act" that allows an
individual to unilaterally discharge federal or state "public debt" using a
private "Exemption Priority." The treatise's claim that individuals on the
"Private side of the accounting cycle" can discharge public debt is not how
federal or state accounting or tax law works. You cannot pay a debt with a
"debt instrument" in the sense the treatise describes.

**Verdict:** **False.** The underlying legal instruments are real, but the
claimed effect (discharge of public debt) is not what they do.

## 9. "The court is looking for an acceptance/acceptor under UCC 3-410"

**Source cited:** UCC § 3-410.

**What's true:** UCC § 3-410 governs presentment for acceptance of a draft.
It is real and specific to negotiable instruments.

**What's not true:** No court "looks for an acceptor" in a criminal case.
An indictment is not presented for acceptance under UCC 3-410. The "acceptor"
is a party who signs a draft to agree to pay it. A state or court cannot be
an "acceptor" of its own charging document. This is a **false analogy**.
Courts have rejected this theory in sovereign-citizen and "statute = bond"
litigation.

**Verdict:** **False.** UCC 3-410 applies to negotiable instruments; it does
not apply to criminal charging documents.

---

## 10. "The U.S. District Court buys all State Court Judgments"

**Source cited:** Seminar transcript (cites searching U.S. Courts → 11th
Circuit).

**What's true:** This is a claim, not a finding. There is no evidence that
federal district courts buy state criminal judgments, and no mechanism in
law requiring them to do so.

**What's not true:** The claim is asserted in the seminar without citation or
documentation. The path Keating gives (search U.S. Courts → 11th Circuit →
Circuit 7 → Illinois Northern District → Clerk's Office → Administrative
Services → Financial Department → "Criminal Justice Act" / "Optional Bids" /
"List of Sureties") is proposed to "verify" it, but tracing that menu does
not establish that the court purchases judgments.

**Verdict:** **Unverified / likely false.** The 11th Circuit navigation is
used as the method of "verification," but the underlying claim is not
established by that navigation alone.

## 11. "The 11th Circuit hosts Treasury 'List of Sureties' and 'Admitted
Reinsure' pages (FMS.Treas.gov)"

**Source cited:** Seminar transcript; Keating's recommended navigation.

**What's true:** Treasury's **Bureau of the Fiscal Service** maintains a
"List of Admitted Reinsurers" and "List of Certified Companies" under
**Department Circular 570**. FMS.Treas.gov is a real Treasury portal. It
issues bonds and has surety/reinsurance relationships with the federal
government.

**What's not true:** Keating's claim is that the **11th Circuit Court of
Appeals** (or the federal court system's Financial Department) publishes or
hosts the "Admitted Reinsure" list and the "List of Sureties." The federal
courts' Financial Department publishes court financial/admin materials, but
the Treasury's admitted-reinsurer and certified-company lists are Treasury
Bureau of the Fiscal Service documents, not court documents. The "11th
Circuit" is used as a *wayfinding hint* to reach the Treasury pages, not as
host. The claim that these are "in the 11th Circuit" is a misreading of the
navigation advice.

**Verdict:** **Partially true / misattributed.** The documents exist; the
location attribution to the 11th Circuit is inaccurate.

## 12. "The state's Retirement Fund / prison system funds global terrorism
(†DivestTerror.Org)"

**Source cited:** www.DivestTerror.Org.

**What's true:** The website exists in Keating's corpus. It is a 115-page
treatise in his own style.

**What's not true:** This is a nationalist/commercial theory with no
sustainable evidentiary support. It links the prison system and state
retirement funds to global terrorism financing without a verifiable, peer-
reviewed, or legally established causal chain. It is outside the scope of
mainstream legal or financial analysis and should not be treated as a
verified claim.

**Verdict:** **Unsubstantiated.** Consistent with other extra-legal claims in
the corpus; not supported by verifiable evidence.

---

## 13. "Every time you sign a check you create a negotiable instrument; every
endorsement makes you an accommodation party under UCC 3-419"

**Source cited:** UCC § 3-419; Black's Law Dictionary.

**What's true:** UCC Article 3 does govern negotiable instruments, and § 3-419
does define "accommodation party" and "accommodation maker."

**What's not true:** The leap from "checking is commercial paper" to "the
courts are suing you for debt collection under assumpsit" is a category
error. A check is a financial instrument; a criminal case is a
government-prosecuted matter. The treatise's framing collapses them, but
courts do not. And the claim that a person can "not go to prison for writing
checks" by refusing to perform is not how commercial dishonor or judicial
wrongful conviction law works.

**Verdict:** **False as applied to criminal courts.** Related to real UCC
concepts but collapsed into a false criminal-commercial system claim.
