# Incarceration Incentive Structure — Claim Entry

**Claim:** Mass incarceration is driven by a cluster of overlapping, documented structural incentives — not a single hidden conspiracy. The system runs on pretrial detention, jail bed leasing, prosecutorial/judicial metrics, policy legacy, and political incentives that reinforce each other.

**Verified Facts:**
- **Pretrial detention & money bail:** Roughly 60–70% of local jail inmates are unconvicted. Bail is money-based, not risk-based, so poor people are held pretrial simply due to inability to pay. (Bureau of Justice Statistics, Vera Institute, National Association of Counties)
- **Per-diem bed leasing:** Many counties operate jails as net cost centers, but generate revenue by leasing beds to out-of-town jurisdictions (states, federal government, immigration). When demand exceeds capacity, operational pressure favors keeping beds full and utilization high. This is a fiscal incentive, not a profit motive from crime.
- **Prosecutor & judicial metrics:** Plea bargaining resolves ~90%+ of cases. Prosecutors face career incentives tied to caseloads, convictions, and sentencing statistics. Judges manage heavy dockets and often default to bail/incarceration for risk management. These are professional incentives, not personal corruption.
- **Policy legacy & underfunding:** Mandatory minimums, three-strikes laws, War on Drugs, and chronic underfunding of mental health treatment, drug courts, and community supervision created a system where incarceration became the default response to social problems.
- **Political incentives:** "Tough on crime" has been a durable political signal. Prosecutors, legislators, and district attorneys gain electoral capital from it. This is structural, not a single actor's plot.

**Interpretation / Context:**
- The claim that there is a single hidden motive is not supported by public records. The incentives are decentralized, often conflicting, and embedded in institutional staffing, funding formulas, and political campaigns.
- "Profit from crime" is often used loosely. Private prisons hold only ~8–10% of the U.S. prison population. Public prisons and jails are taxpayer-funded. The real revenue engine is pretrial detention and bed leasing, not criminal activity itself.
- These incentives persist because they are embedded in how courts, counties, and agencies are staffed, funded, and measured — not because of a coordinated plan.

**Status:** Verified against public records (BJS, Vera Institute, Sentencing Project, ACLU, national court/clerk reports). Structural drivers confirmed; single-conspiracy framing rejected.

**Sources:** Bureau of Justice Statistics jail population reports; Vera Institute for Justice pretrial detention resources; Sentencing Project mandatory minimums/threestrikes research; National Association of Counties jail operations; ACLU mass incarceration reports; GAO/CRS studies on jail finances and bed leasing.
